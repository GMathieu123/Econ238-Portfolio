# NOTICE!

## Our Classrooms Are Not for Sale

**A proposed cultural moat for the University of Rochester**

**The proposal.** For future classrooms and teaching buildings, Rochester should stop exchanging naming rights for donations. Existing agreements would stay intact. Donors could still receive recognition in a public registry.

**What are we resisting?** Universities face pressure to compete for prestige and private funding. This rule would reject the idea that money can buy a permanent name on an academic space. It binds administrators to a visible principle: classrooms belong to the university community, not to a donor.

**What would it cost?** The most obvious cost is lost naming-related gifts, borne first by university fundraising. But the biggest cost could be delayed construction, fewer scholarships, or reduced programs—ultimately affecting students. Possible benefits include public trust, institutional independence, and a stronger identity. None is guaranteed.

**What happens without it?** Rochester continues its existing naming policy and keeps access to potentially valuable gifts, but remains subject to the pressures of donor recognition. A cheaper alternative would be stricter donor screening and shorter naming terms.

**The question:** Is protecting the university's independence worth sacrificing money that could support students?

*Economic basis: [University of Rochester Institutional Naming Policy](https://www.rochester.edu/policies/policy/institutional-naming-policy/) (revised May 2025). This is a proposal, not current policy.*
